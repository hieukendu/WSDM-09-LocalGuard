## Part B. Method summaries

### 1. ENOUGH (arXiv 2609.08180)

What is selected. For each request, an ordered and variable-length subset of a user's retrieved history records (the profile), drawn from the top-M pool of a frozen retriever, possibly empty. What is minimized is the incremental prompt-token cost of the profile subject to keeping the utility attainable from the pool. Nothing in the paper concerns privacy or sensitive content. The word "privacy" does not occur in the method or the evaluation.

Datasets and metrics. LaMP time-based split, tasks LaMP-1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7. Accuracy and F1 (LaMP-1, 2), MAE and RMSE (LaMP-3), ROUGE-1 and ROUGE-L (LaMP-4, 5, 7). Frozen generators Qwen3.5-9B (main) and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Baselines BM25, Contriever, IC-RALM, REPLUG, RankGPT, ICR, RSPG-Pre, PURPLE, all at K=5 from the same M=20 pool. Efficiency is reported as profile tokens, root Stop rate, harm rate relative to the empty profile, generator calls and latency.

Guarantee. None that is statistical. Proposition 1 ("exact-value stopping") states that greedy argmax over the exact Q values with ties resolved toward Stop returns a J-optimal terminal profile on the finite deterministic candidate tree. The paper itself says this is conditional on access to exact Q*, which the learned controller only approximates. There is no conformal, PAC or risk-control guarantee on anything, and no exposure metric.

Selection rule, in enough detail to reimplement a token-cost gate in the ENOUGH style.

- Candidate pool. C_i = top-M records from a frozen retriever (Contriever), M = 20. Maximum depth K_i = min(K_max, |C_i|) with K_max = 10. Hard context limit L_max on the rendered prompt. Records that would overflow are masked, never truncated.
- Action space at step t. Append one unused feasible record d to the ordered profile S_t, or emit Stop. Starting state is the empty profile, so Stop at the root (no personalization) is allowed.
- Token cost (Eq. 8). c_i(S) = [Tok(P(x_i, S)) - Tok(P(x_i, empty))] / L_ref, where Tok is the generator's own tokenizer count of the full rendered prompt and L_ref is a "profile reference budget shared by all tasks". The cost is real incremental tokens, not record count.
- Task gain (Eq. 11-13). l_i(S) = length-normalized log-likelihood of the reference output y_i under the frozen generator given the prompt with profile S. g_i(S) = l_i(S) - l_i(empty). Normalized by a per-task scale s_tau^g = sqrt(E[g^2]) computed on a fixed calibration subset, with no mean subtraction so that g(empty) = 0.
- User specificity (Eq. 14-18). For slot r, Delta_u = l(S) - l(S without d_r), and Delta_c,k = l(S without d_r, plus matched other-user record m_k) - l(S without d_r) for K^- = 3 content-matched records from other users. rho = [Delta_u]_+ - mean_k [Delta_c,k]_+. p_i(S) = 1[g_i(S) > 0] * mean over slots of rho, estimated with one uniformly sampled slot per profile, then scale-normalized like g.
- Objective. Cost-free utility U_i(S) = g_bar(S) + beta * p_bar(S) (Eq. 19). Cost-regularized surrogate J_i^lambda(S) = U_i(S) - lambda * c_i(S) (Eq. 7). The epsilon-minimal sufficient profile (Eq. 6) is argmin c_i(S) subject to U_i(S) >= U_i* - epsilon. lambda traces the quality-cost frontier.
- Value recursion (Eq. 20-22). V*(S) = max{ J^lambda(S), max_d V*(S + d) }, Q*(s, Stop) = J^lambda(S), Q*(s, d) = V*(S + d).
- Offline supervision. A bounded prefix tree per training instance: roots sampled from the empty profile, retriever prefixes, recency, diversity and random subsets, stratified over sizes 0..K_i. At roots, Stop and all feasible adds are expanded. Deeper, an action budget J = 8 (mix of top retriever score, diversity, uniform) with beam width W = 4 per depth. Each (s, a) is labelled with the three components (R^g, R^p, R^c) of the J-maximizing leaf in its subtree, and R^net = R^g + beta R^p - lambda R^c. One extra policy-induced aggregation round (DAgger-like) adds states visited by the trained controller.
- Controller. A three-head value network Q^g, Q^p, Q^c over state features (query encoding, order-sensitive profile encoding, attention pool over remaining candidates, and budget features: current incremental token cost, remaining horizon, remaining context budget). Q^net = Q^g + beta Q^p - lambda Q^c (Eq. 23). Loss = Huber value regression + eta * listwise KL ranking loss (softmax with temperature T_r) + zeta * BCE stop loss on the margin with temperature T_s, with eta = 0.2 and zeta = 0.4. The stop loss is applied only on fully expanded states.
- Inference and stopping rule (Eq. 24, 32). At each step compute Q^net for every feasible add and for Stop. Stop margin Delta_stop(s) = Q^net(s, Stop) - max_d Q^net(s, d). Stop if and only if Delta_stop >= 0 (ties go to Stop), or when no feasible action remains (depth K_max or context limit). Otherwise append argmax_d Q^net(s, d). The generator is called once after stopping.
- Operating point. (beta, lambda) = (0.4, 0.10), chosen on development data. lambda = 0.10 cut mean profile length from 741 to 512 tokens (30.9 percent) against lambda = 0 at nearly unchanged quality.
- Not recoverable from the HTML text. The numeric values of L_ref, L_max, T_r, T_s and the Huber weights w_k are not stated in the prose that could be extracted (they may sit in a table rendered as an image). A reimplementation must choose them and say so.

A faithful light-weight gate for a baseline row would therefore be: greedy sequential admission over the top-20 pool, score each candidate by a predicted net value (predicted gain plus 0.4 times specificity minus 0.10 times incremental tokens over L_ref), stop when the value of stopping is at least the best add value, cap at 10 records. If no trained controller is available, the honest label is "ENOUGH-style token-cost gate (reimplemented, myopic scorer)", since the paper's own ablation distinguishes the long-horizon controller from a myopic one.

### 2. PURPLE (arXiv 2601.12078, ACL 2026)

PURPLE selects and orders K = 5 user history records out of a pool of 20 retrieved by Contriever, with the aim of maximizing generation quality, not of minimizing anything. Selection is cast as a contextual bandit whose policy is a Plackett-Luce ranking model over records, built on an encoder with token-level cross-attention and inter-record dependency modelling. The policy is trained with the likelihood-ratio (REINFORCE) gradient, z-score normalizing the rewards of the sampled profiles for each example. The reward is the log-likelihood that the frozen LLM assigns to the reference personalized response. At inference the top-K records by propensity score are taken, so the profile length is fixed. Evaluation covers nine tasks, six from LaMP and three from LongLaMP, with accuracy, F1, MAE, RMSE, ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L and METEOR, using Phi-4-Mini-Instruct, Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Llama-3-70B-Instruct as frozen generators. There is no privacy or exposure metric of any kind and no finite-sample or distribution-free guarantee. The only theory is an appendix argument that the objective corresponds to an ELBO of marginalization-based RAG.

### 3. MINIM (arXiv 2606.13949, ICML 2026)

MINIM minimizes the UI observation (accessibility-tree nodes) that an autonomous GUI or web agent transmits to a remote inference server. It does not select personal records for a prompt. A trusted local model predicts two scalars per node, an inherent sensitivity score s and a task-conditioned necessity score n. A fixed ternary policy then removes a node if n is below tau_nec, abstracts it (placeholder for the sensitive attribute) if n is above tau_nec and s is above tau_sens, and keeps it otherwise. The scorer is trained with a contextual-integrity-aware loss that weights necessity errors more heavily on high-sensitivity content. Data is a privacy-augmented WebArena corpus (Shopping, Reddit, Gmail): 150 accessibility trees, 27 task types, 5,403 tree-task variants with injected synthetic sensitive context such as 2FA codes. Metrics are TISL (task-irrelevant sensitive leakage, normalized to the full observation, 0.101 for MINIM), TCNP (context recall, 0.949) and TCNP-I (actionable recall, 0.993), with a keep rate near 12 percent. The thresholds are fixed defaults that "administrators can adjust". They are not calibrated. No finite-sample or distribution-free guarantee is given on leakage. The appendix offers only an information-bottleneck motivation.

### 4. Zhou, Mireshghallah, Li (arXiv 2510.03662, ICLR 2026)

The unit of minimization is each detected sensitive span inside a single user prompt, which receives one of three ordered actions: RETAIN, ABSTRACT or REDACT. The paper defines the data-minimization optimum as the most privacy-preserving action profile whose response still passes a utility predicate for a given response model. It finds that optimum by a two-stage search. Stage 1 probes each span alone and freezes spans whose redaction and abstraction both break utility. Stage 2 runs a priority-queue tree search that starts from the most redacted variant and relaxes one action at a time in order of increasing disclosure, the order being given by a privacy comparator (a LoRA-finetuned Qwen2.5-7B distilled from o3 labels, 71 percent agreement with humans overall and 89 percent on high-consensus items). It returns the first variant that passes the utility check. Datasets are ShareGPT and WildChat (open-ended) and CaseHold and MedQA (single ground truth), with nine response models. Utility is judged by GPT-4o or the task scorer, with k = 5 repeated decodes for single-answer QA. The privacy measure is the share of REDACT, ABSTRACT and RETAIN actions (85.7 percent redaction for GPT-5 against 19.3 percent for Qwen2.5-0.5B), backed by two attacker audits (type-wise Hit@1 and Hit@3, and span-wise recovery) with Llama-3.1-70B and Gemini Flash 1.5 as adversaries. A second experiment shows that LLMs asked to predict the minimized prompt directly are biased toward ABSTRACT and overshare. The result is a per-instance search oracle that needs the response model in the loop. There is no calibration set, no held-out guarantee and no conformal, PAC or risk-control statement on exposure.

### 6. SP-Mem (arXiv 2608.16551)

SP-Mem is a memory architecture for conversational agents that separates searchable sanitized memory from a protected mapping to exact private values. At write time an LLM pipeline identifies PII and stores sanitized content and exact values in isolated structures (vector plus graph memory with a privacy mapping layer). At query time an LLM reasoning step decides whether the task needs exact private values. If it does, the system asks for user consent and restores only the task-required values. Otherwise it answers from sanitized memory. All decisions are prompted LLM judgments. Nothing is trained with a guarantee and no threshold is calibrated. The evaluation uses the authors' own synthetic benchmark: 1,000 synthesized user profiles over finance, medical, education and mental support, of which 100 users are sampled, giving 2,100 history dialogues and 5,400 queries in five task categories (preference-only, privacy-only allowed or denied, mixed allowed or denied). Baselines are full context, Zep, Mem0 and MemOS. Quality metrics are pairwise task completion (P-TC) and personalization quality (P-PQ) judged by an LLM. Privacy metrics are Privacy-Appropriate Requesting (PAR: accuracy, precision, recall, with precision 1.00 and recall 0.84-0.92) and Unnecessary Privacy Usage (UPU), a rule-based binary check for exact private values in responses when they are unnecessary or unauthorized. Token use is 26-31 percent of full context. The exposure metric is purely empirical on synthetic data and the limitations section says it covers only exact-value exposure. There is no finite-sample or distribution-free guarantee.

### 10. Conformal Arbitrage (arXiv 2506.00911, NeurIPS 2025)

Conformal Arbitrage is a router between a Primary model, tuned for a primary objective, and a more conservative Guardian (another model or a human). Given the Primary's scores over a finite action set, it forms a lambda-relaxed candidate set of actions whose score is within lambda of the top. If the set is a singleton the Primary acts. Otherwise the slate is deferred to the Guardian. The threshold lambda-hat is calibrated with conformal risk control on an exchangeable calibration sample, using a monotone loss equal to the residual guardrail risk of the Guardian-best action inside the candidate set, so that the expected guardrail loss is at most alpha in finite samples and without distributional assumptions. Theorem 1 adds that no other score-gap router on the Primary's scores attains strictly higher expected primary utility at the same budget, up to an O(1/n) term. Experiments cover cost against accuracy on TruthfulQA and MMLU and helpfulness against harmlessness on PKU-SafeRLHF. The controlled risks are factual error and harmfulness. Privacy, PII and personal-data exposure are never the controlled risk, and the unit of exchangeability is the query, not the user. It is the closest methodological template (one scalar threshold, CRC, monotone loss) but it does not address data minimization or record admission.

### 11. Chakraborty et al. (arXiv 2511.17908, ECIR 2026)

The paper filters retrieved context before generation. Retrieved documents are cut into 500-character snippets, each snippet gets a nonconformity score A(q, s) (one minus the cosine similarity of Qwen3-Embedding-8B embeddings, or one minus a GPT-4o relevance rating), and a snippet is kept if and only if its score is at most a threshold. The threshold is the split-conformal (1 - alpha) quantile, the ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))-th smallest score among the n labelled-relevant calibration snippets, with topic-disjoint calibration and test splits. The guarantee is marginal coverage of relevant evidence: P(s kept | s relevant) >= 1 - alpha under exchangeability. It is a recall guarantee on what is retained. Collections are NeuCLIR and RAGTIME. Metrics are empirical coverage, context reduction (two to three times) and downstream ARGUE F1 on NeuCLIR. The paper uses plain split conformal prediction, not conformal risk control, and exchangeability is at the snippet level, not the user or query-group level. Nothing concerns privacy, sensitive content or personal data. Its guarantee is the mirror image of what LocalGuard needs: it lower-bounds retention of good content, whereas LocalGuard upper-bounds admission of sensitive content.

## Part C. Has conformal risk control already been applied to privacy exposure or record admission?

Verdict. Within the limits of these searches, no prior work calibrates a record-admission (or memory-admission) threshold on held-out users so that expected audited sensitive exposure is at most alpha under user-level exchangeability. The combination of (a) personal-record admission into an LLM prompt, (b) an audited sensitive-exposure loss, (c) conformal risk control and (d) exchangeability over users appears open as of 2026-10-05. The idea is nevertheless a direct instantiation of known CRC machinery (Angelopoulos et al. 2024, with Conformal Arbitrage and CORA as close templates), and the area is moving fast: three of the nearest neighbours appeared between April and September 2026. The novelty claim should therefore be phrased narrowly ("to our knowledge the first to give a finite-sample bound on audited sensitive exposure for personal-record admission in retrieval-augmented personalization"), and CORA, Conformal Privacy Auditing, Role-Stratified CRC and OCELOT should be cited and separated explicitly. Two caveats on coverage: Semantic Scholar and DBLP could not be queried, and a paper that words the idea without "conformal" (for example "PAC privacy budget for memory retrieval") could have been missed. Rerun the search before submission.
