\section{Scope and interpretation}
All disclosure measurements are relative to Qwen and Gemma auditors, not
human-validated privacy or harm rates. The bound concerns expected profile
exposure for an exchangeable user. Counting the always-sent query as one
record yields $E_{\rm total}=E_{\rm profile}+a(q)$ and hence an expected
bound of $\alpha+1$, because $0\leq a(q)\leq1$; query disclosure prevalence
was not measured. A query-only prompt is not a zero-loss total-prompt fallback.

\section{Severity sensitivity and repeated disclosures}
The exploratory severity loss assigns 2 to a record with any health,
mental-health, sexual-orientation, race/nationality or finance union flag,
1 to another flagged record, and 0 otherwise. Overlapping categories use
the maximum weight. With a five-record cap the loss bound is 10.
Weights are illustrative policy choices, not a validated harm scale or
legal classification. At the same numerical target the units differ from
uniform counts. The table uses 300 matched random calibration/test halves,
the original cleared-rule grid and union labels; numbers are average
held-out losses, not confidence bounds. No utility conclusion follows
from these admission counts.

Real multi-query cumulative distinct disclosures are not measured. The
artifact contains a limited synthetic sensitivity: independently shuffled
rankings of the same audited top-eight pool for up to ten arrivals, with
the original query-specific scores fixed. On LaMP-3 at $\alpha=0.5$,
single-prompt calibration produces \CumulativeSingleOne{} unique flagged records at one
arrival and \CumulativeSingleTen{} at ten; calibrating the cumulative loss directly gives
\CumulativeCalibratedTen{} at ten. There are no new queries, retrieval beyond the fixed pool,
or measured utility. These results do not establish safety for adaptive
interaction. Linearity of expectation gives a $T\alpha$ bound on record
occurrences only when each prompt's marginal assumptions remain valid;
distinct records are at most those occurrences, with no individual-user guarantee.

\section{Human audit status}
No human annotations have been completed. Two independent blind annotators
and human adjudication are required before reporting precision/recall or a
human-calibrated operating point. The existing 120-record stratified packet
has Gemma coverage on only 96 records and contains no fully labelled user
pool. Its descriptive metrics cannot identify an exact operating-point
shift. Missing labels are bounded, never treated as negative labels.
Full top-eight pools from disjoint calibration and test users are required;
scores and the threshold grid must remain frozen. The private annotation
workflow checks category consistency, independence attestations and
adjudication. Model repeatability below does not replace human validation.
A second prepared packet covers all eight candidates of 60 uniformly sampled
LaMP-3 users (480 records per annotator), with 30 calibration and 30 disjoint
test users fixed before annotation. Rule and grid hashes prevent changes
during this study. It can identify a small-cohort operating-point shift after
genuine annotation; it currently contains zero labels and provides no human result.

\section{Auditor and judge reproducibility}
Each auditor receives one displayed record per call. Record text is truncated
at 60 words for LaMP-3, 40 for LaMP-7 and 90 for LaMP-QA; LaMP-3 retains its
rating prefix. Both auditors use greedy decoding (temperature 0), seed 0,
a 40-token output limit and JSON output. Qwen is served by Ollama 0.30.8;
Gemma uses its bundled llama.cpp server with the same local GGUF weights,
\texttt{--jinja}, an 8192-token total context and four slots. The original
parser filters category names and maps malformed replies to an empty list;
repeat agreement therefore measures the complete model-and-parser pipeline.
The second judge uses Gemma with the same rubric prompt and 40-token limit
as the Qwen judge. Scores are clamped to 0--2, truncated to the rubric length,
missing entries padded with zero, and malformed replies scored zero.
